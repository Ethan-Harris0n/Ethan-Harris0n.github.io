gap year log

I decided not to take/search for a job after graduating. I thought it would be cool to log what a year off from career looks like that I can return to when considering a similar choice in the future. This is somewhat stream of consciousness and some of it was from memory as I stayed away from laptop for periods.

May 26th-June 18th:

  • Completed School of Rock with MITOC outing club.

  • The goal of the program which included climbing three times a week (weekends outside and one gym session on Thursdays) and Tuesday instructionals on theory is to become a competent outside climber who can reliably manage safety for yourself and for a group. I completed this mainly to be able to climb outside competently for an upcoming national parks road trip.

  • The remainder of June and July was spent doing some minor trips. Learning how to do nothing again. And playing lots of volleyball and climbing…

June 20th-June 24th:

  • I spent the weekend exploring Coastal Maine and Portland. A city I had never visited. Good happy hours, and some great food. If I ever live in the US Portland, Maine is probably at the top of the list (food, outdoors, not too big / not too small)

June 25th-July 1st:

  • Said goodbye to three different friends this week. Celebrated memories in Cambridge for the last three years. Slept a lot. Read even more.

July 1st-4th:

  • Lots of networking and job calls as I begin to start thinking about what’s next career wise. Feeling a little torn between continued vagabonding versus settling into a normal career. I think there’s definitely a need to get out of Cambridge and do some real thinking.

July 4th:

  • Held backyard fix it party for fourth of July. Repaired our Picnic table and watched fireworks.

July 9th:

  • Held Potluck and clothing swap. Built bed platform for cross-country road trip.

July 13th:

  • Flew to France to renovate the house.

July 13th-18th:

  • Read GK Chesterton’s Everlasting man and drank a lot of natural wine.

July 18th-August 13th:

  • 3 books.

  • An awesome hike in pyrenees and visits to swimming holes with gf.

  • Notably, August 12th after taking a break away from laptop and doing lots of thinking — landed on some long-term career desires and I think I might want to pursue a PhD. I think being in nature often seems to give clarity on what’s next.

August 13th-19th:

Fin.

Some end of year notes (written off and on — finalized late July - 2026).

  • We never get everything done we think we can. I thought I would accomplish a lot more than I did. But I think prioritizing rest and exploration of different interests / hobbies / personal projects didn’t align in the way I’d hoped at times.
  • Making rest a priority is really really (really) hard in today’s world. I still feel I failed in this respect.
  • Balancing the desire for rest and relaxation with wanting to do things (even non-career things is hard). Hyper-productivity orientation / psychology — the instincts for success in American high performing circles are not just a switch I am able to flip. This is disappointing and I wish I could figure it out. I still feel I failed in this respect of not doing as many things as I wanted to do?
  • How do I both feel like I haven’t rested enough but also that I don’t do enough?
  • It’s hard to go back to the way we were — removing work / school and moving to a new environment did not make me go back to my late teens / early 20s carefree surfer self. Instead it’s like I see it in glimpses, and those glimpses increased over time — a promise of what could be. Kind of good thing — I wouldn’t trade the growth and maturity I’ve gained for the absolute confidence and carefreeness I had in prior seasons, I would however like to map the mean of the two a little more successfully, to take the good of my youth (confidence, joy, curiosity, mischievousness) with leaving the bad behind (arrogance, words without consideration for others, mischievousness without discernment). As of yet I haven’t been able to do that. How to live the embodied self I was when I was young, with the analytical maturity that mistakes and age have brought.
  • Hard to know but interesting that when traveling solo or not in a fixed group — the beauty of it is what goes wrong. In a group, that sometimes seems to shift. The serendipitous aspects of travel and what makes it great is harder to create unless the group really jells.
  • Other gap year things discovered / some numbers:
    • Bikepacking was a fun discovery.
    • Love outdoor climbing and think I will be doing it for a long time.
    • Like the dirtbag life as much in my late twenties as I did in my teens.
    • Being nomadic feels…right. But France is a great spot to call home base and can’t wait to live there for a year sometime again.
    • Some of the best times of the year was when I was not doing anything. But felt weird about how this felt almost…immoral. Something to figure out.
    • Love building life around the outdoors – there doesn’t seem to be any decline of fulfillment even when it was all I was doing for a period – will continue to be a big part of my life.
    • Being on the road feels more personally who I am. idk.
    • Books, family, some exercise and the feeling of building something tangible is really all I need to be content.
