random reading recs

(a compilation of WhatsApp texts)

  1. Wind in the Willows — Wisdom in levity.
  2. Tolstoy’s A Confession — Timeless in its conclusion and analysis of modern life.
  3. Ecclesiastes from the Hebrew bible — Most beautiful text I’ve ever read.
  4. Fear and Trembling by Soren Kierkegard — Faith (read GK Chesterton’s Everlasting Man for additional notes).
  5. The Denial of Death by Earnest Becker — The danger of our good intentions, what we’re all running from in life, and why / where evil originates in man.
  6. East of Eden by John Steinbeck — On allowing oneself to heal / the redeeming nature of life.
  7. Braiding Sweetgrass by Robin Wall Kemmer — Anti-consumption treatise that is easy to consume.
  8. An Everlasting Meal by Tamar Adler — Food (and cooking) will never be the same again. Read it every year in the first couple weeks of January.
  9. Total Freedom by Judda Krishnamurti — Good for a cliche Eat/Pray/Love phase of life.
  10. Celebration of Discipline by Richard Foster. — Read alongside the Divine Conspiracy. His chapter on joy and jubilation is immense.
  11. The Divine Conspiracy by Dallas Willard — Strips away doctrine “down to the studs.” Challenges the doctrines and preconceptions that often turn people away from it.
  12. Paul by N.T. Wright — Additional understanding on the underlying tenants of the Christian faith by a leading scholar.
  13. Essential Writings of Thich Knat Hanh — Excellent.
  14. Act of Creation by Koestler — All 600 pages are packed with awesome notes on creativity, humour, and developmental psychology.
  15. The Wayfinders by Wade Davis — Anthropological critique of modernity and progress. Like it for its succinctness. Silent Spring / Limits to Growth triggered similar thoughts but are denser and more climate focused. Whereas this highlights general environmental, cultural, and relational destruction due to the influence of western thought and western conceptions of growth and progress. Makes you consider the underlying problematic fundamentals of western values and culture.
  16. Lord of The Rings by JR Tolkien — Epic yarns and lessons on hope.
  17. Zorba the Greek by Nikos Kazantzakis — More lessons on levity.
  18. Four Loves by C.S. Lewis – Friendship
  19. Hawk and the Dove trilogy / Hannah Coulter by Wendell Berry — Notes on grief and changing seasons
  20. An Everlasting Man by GK Chesterton — Stuffy but some banger quotes that address many secular views and challenges to accepting christianity. Written a while ago but somehow perfectly addresses the rationality / scientific method point of view that can make it hard to accept religion. I would also recommend his book orthodoxy for thought provoking quips on dangers of modern thought around rationality, free will, and determinism.
  21. Winners take all — Ranty at times, but useful if you find yourself in elite circles (especially on the American coasts), and / or are navigating the early career tension of doing good and acheiving financial security.
  22. The Dispossessed by Le Guin – Will pick up anything by her – good poetry too.
  23. Brothers by Dostefsky — Just read it
  24. Need for Roots by Simone Weil — Thoughts on how to rebuild and regrow a society that has taken a wrong turn.
  25. Why We Swim – I find myself recommending this book often and can’t say exactly why.
  26. Leisure: The Basis of Culture by Josef Pieper — notes on pace and space – might lead to a gap year
