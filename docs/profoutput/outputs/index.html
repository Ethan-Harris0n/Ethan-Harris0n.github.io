Migration

Forecasting Conflict-Induced Migration and Displacement in Yemen

My undergraduate thesis leveraged Machine-Learning to forecast the movements of internally-displaced persons in Yemen at a sub-national (ADM2) level. The core idea of this project was that if we can forecast where people will migrate following a conflict event, we can pre-position resources and better target aid to these populations – thereby optimizing humanitarian aid allocation. Conflict Prediction

During my time at the UNDP Crisis Bureau, I built a conflict prediction framework that leveraged machine-learning to forecast conflict risk sub-nationally. The design of this framework differed from many traditional conflict prediction applications in that it focused on forecasting relative conflict risk at highly granular spatial (ADM2) and temporal level (Bi-Weekly) rather than predicting the onset of conflict. A brief I wrote during grad-school highlighting the approach and a general discussion on predictive analytics for humanitarian response can be found here. The code leverages conformal prediction and time-series feature generation methods to build conflict forecasting models with promising results in validation (~87% accuracy).

Development / Economics

The Impact of Mobile Phones on Women’s Economic Empowerment in Rural East Africa.

May-December of 2017, I worked as a Program Manager for Phillip Roessler and Innovations for Poverty Action in Tanzania to implement a series of Randomized Control Trials examining the impact of mobile phones on women’s economic empowerment. In these studies, we found that the impact of mobile phones translated to an economic impact that was far greater than the cost of the phone itself. The final published work can be found here

Impact of Community Policing on Citizen Trust and Social Cohesion in Medellin, Colombia

On assignment in Summer 2017, I supported the implementation of an RCT in Medellin, Colombia to examine the impact of community policing on citizen trust in police and social cohesion.
